Grounded Wings · The Earliest Flightless Bird: Exploring *Yuezhuornis yangyuanensis* and the mosaic evolution of avian body plans. On October 14, 2026, the journal Nature published a major paleontological discovery led by Min Wang at the Institute of Vertebrate Paleontology and Paleoanthropology (IVPP), Chinese Academy of Sciences, with the Fujian Institute of Geological Sciences. The team described a ~148–150 million-year-old fossil bird found in August 2024 near Yangyuan village, Zhenghe County, Fujian Province, China. Its generic name stems from Yuezhuo, a legendary Chinese bird of the phoenix family.

Crow-sized (about 0.5 m tall and just over 800 g), this early avialan broke the conventional flight mould: its forelimbs were shorter than its hindlimbs (resembling modern ground-dwelling pheasants), contrasting sharply with the contemporary Late Jurassic Archaeopteryx whose forelimbs were longer than its hindlimbs. Anatomical comparisons with nearly 400 living bird species reveal an evolutionary mosaic combining a bird-like pelvis and hindlimbs with dinosaurian traits including closely packed serrated teeth and a hyper-extensible second toe. Reasoned inferences suggest it thrived as a swift ground runner in a swampy environment—much like a modern roadrunner—flying only in short bursts when necessary, making it the earliest known bird to lose continuous flight.

Lead researcher Min Wang said, "Yuezhuornis challenges the traditional paradigm that the avian body plan evolved almost exclusively under the selective pressures of flight."
