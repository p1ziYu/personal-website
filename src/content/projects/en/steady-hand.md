Steady Hand · Taming Boom and Bust. Honoring the 2026 Sveriges Riksbank Prize in Economic Sciences in Memory of Alfred Nobel awarded to Robert E. Hall and Michael Woodford "for microeconomic foundations of macroeconomic stabilisation".

When aggregate Keynesian levers collapsed under 1970s stagflation, Hall and Woodford rebuilt macroeconomic policy on forward-looking microfoundations. Households smooth consumption across their lifespans based on expected permanent income; firms facing Calvo price stickiness set prices looking toward the future. Because expectations dictate outcomes, monetary policy operates primarily through communication: credible forward guidance anchors inflation with minimal output loss.

This interactive seismograph models dynamic IS curves, forward-looking Phillips curves, and central-bank policy levers to reveal how modern stabilization policy softens economic shocks.
