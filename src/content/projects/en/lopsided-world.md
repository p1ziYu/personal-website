LOPSIDED WORLD · WASP-17 b — Exploring the astronomical milestone published on October 14, 2026 in *Nature Astronomy* (DOI: 10.1038/s41550-026-02986-4, title "A Spatial Map of the Atmospheric Limb of a Hot Jupiter"): using the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST), an international team produced the most detailed spatial map yet of an exoplanet's atmospheric limb, discovering that the hot Jupiter WASP-17 b is not a perfect circle, but a distinctly lopsided world.

Every planet you have ever seen illustrated is a pristine circle — this one is not.

### Core Discoveries & Research Team

WASP-17 b is a "hot Jupiter" located approximately 1,300 light-years from Earth. It is a large, puffy gas giant orbiting scorching close to its host star, with no solid surface whatsoever. Tidally locked by stellar gravity, one hemisphere permanently faces the stellar furnace (permanent dayside) while the opposite side endures eternal darkness (permanent nightside).

The research was led jointly by Cornell University and the University of Bristol. The lead author is Lili Alderson, Klarman Postdoctoral Fellow at Cornell University (who initiated the project during her doctoral research at the University of Bristol). The underlying analysis technique, known as "transmission strings", was developed by Bristol astrophysicists David Grant and Hannah Wakeford.

### The Old View vs. Transmission Strings

- **The Old View**: During a planetary transit across the stellar disk, standard exoplanet models treated the planet's atmospheric annulus as a single uniform circular ring. This generated one limb-averaged spectrum that blended morning limb, evening limb, equator, and polar zones into a single wash, obscuring spatial variation.
- **Transmission Strings**: As different slices of the planetary limb enter and exit the stellar disk during transit ingress and egress, their optical contributions shift dynamically. By fitting these geometry shifts using open-source modeling software Harmonica, the team extracted distinct spectra across different angles along the limb. The team divided the spectra into 24 discrete wavelength intervals to map physical parameters around the perimeter, opening the door to probing equatorial-polar differences as well as morning-evening gradients.

### JWST Observations & Physical Mechanisms

Observations combined three premier JWST instruments — NIRISS, NIRSpec, and MIRI — spanning an unprecedented continuous wavelength window from 0.6 to 12 micrometers under the TST DREAMS program.

Spectroscopic retrieval uncovered profound morning-evening asymmetry:
- **Cooler Morning Limb (Western)**: Atmospheric temperatures sit near ~850 K (modeled around 858 K).
- **Hotter Evening Limb (Eastern)**: Atmospheric temperatures climb to ~1,500 K (modeled around 1,522 K).
- **Thermal Divide & Atmospheric Puffing**: The temperature gap reaches approximately 650 K (equal in Celsius, ~1,170 °F). Because scale height $H = \frac{k_B T}{\mu g}$ scales directly with temperature, the hotter evening gas exhibits a far more gradual density drop-off with altitude, extending about 1.8 times farther into space than the cooler, compact morning atmosphere!
- **Chemical Homogeneity**: In contrast to the temperature divide, water vapor (H₂O) and carbon dioxide (CO₂) abundances remain broadly consistent around the limb, proving that a severe thermal contrast does not cause all chemical ingredients to vary identically.
- **Wind Dynamics**: High-altitude equatorial winds carry scorching gas from the permanent dayside around the evening limb (hot, puffed-up atmosphere). The gas then cools as it traverses the freezing nightside before emerging at the morning limb cooler and more compact. This directly confirms hydrodynamic predictions of global exoplanetary heat circulation.

### Attributed Quotes

Lead author Lili Alderson remarked:
> "Rather than assuming your atmosphere is a circle with the same radius all the way around, our technique allows you to pull out different distances from the centre to the edge of the planet."

Alderson further emphasized:
> "This is proof of concept that planets are in fact 3D objects, and that consistently, as they go around their star, what we predict should be happening is happening."

### Scientific Honesty Note

Atmospheric temperatures cited (~850 K and ~1,500 K) represent model-inferred values retrieved from transmission spectra, not in situ thermometer measurements; they carry model uncertainties, with the cooler morning side less tightly constrained, and are presented as approximate values. All limb-profile graphics and transit diagrams on this page are clearly labeled **schematic illustrations** designed for conceptual clarity, not to-scale astronomical images. WASP-17 b is a scorching gas giant with no solid ground; **do not claim or interpret this planet as habitable**.
