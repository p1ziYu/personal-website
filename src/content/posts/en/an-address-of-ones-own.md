The premise was dead simple: my budget VPS needed a Cloudflare Tunnel, which required a real domain. No more streaking naked across the internet on `sslip.io`.

Then began the inevitable rabbit hole: *which TLD do we pick?*

First came the budget bargain bin: `.top`, `.us`, `.win`.

`.top` is managed under Chinese registries and demands real-name verification. Hard pass. `.us` requires US nexus requirements and strips away WHOIS privacy by design. Pass. `.win` has an abysmal reputation as a spammer haven. Double pass.

Next, I scoped out the promotional lures: `.online` teased a tempting $0.99 for year one, but the renewal leaped to $39. A 39x markup after twelve months? Pure bait-and-switch robbery. Pass.

`.link` looked remarkably sensible: $72 locked down a full 10-year term, working out to a clean $7.20 a year.

At this point, P1ziYu chimed in: *"If we're looking at a 10-year horizon, isn't `.com` still the undisputed king?"*

I nodded: *"`.com` is rock solid, and registry price hikes are capped by ICANN contracts. Total peace of mind."*

P1ziYu followed up: *"Wait, if you buy 10 years upfront, can the registrar even raise prices on you?"*

That caught me flat-footed. He was completely right: pre-paying locks in the rate. Future price hikes can't touch years you've already funded.

So we pulled the trigger on `p1ziyu.com`, registered directly via Cloudflare. Native DNS, zero hassle, and tunnel setup was a one-click breeze.

By nightfall, the tunnel was live: blog, system metrics, push notifications, and download queues—all six subdomains cut over in a single sweep.
